## Design Context

### Users

Experienced software engineers reviewing pull requests across codebases they know partially but not completely. They need to build a defensible understanding of behavioral changes, risk, evidence, and test coverage without surrendering the approval decision to automation.

### Brand Personality

Rigorous, calm, and confidence-building. ReviewArc should feel like a focused review instrument: candid about uncertainty, precise about provenance, and quietly helpful under sustained daily use.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, information-dense desktop workspace inspired by the supplied ReviewArc arc identity and review mockups. Use a cool ink-and-paper foundation with a restrained indigo-to-cyan arc accent, semantic green/amber/red only for state, and code-first layouts that preserve visual hierarchy without card soup. The interface should feel editorial and technical rather than playful, glossy, neon, or dashboard-generic. Support compact desktop widths, keyboard navigation, reduced motion, and high-contrast status cues that do not rely on color alone.

### Design Principles

1. Evidence before assertion: every risk, inference, and unknown should lead back to real code or metadata.
2. Preserve the review arc: orient users from inbox to change summary to guided chapters to a deliberate decision.
3. Dense, never cramped: prioritize scanability, stable panel geometry, and progressive disclosure over decorative whitespace.
4. Make uncertainty visible: distinguish facts, inferences, unsupported claims, stale analysis, and operational errors in both language and presentation.
5. Keep agency explicit: optimistic local progress is appropriate, but publishing feedback always requires a clear confirmation boundary.
